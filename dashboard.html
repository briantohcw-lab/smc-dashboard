CONFLUENCE GUIDE — WHAT EACH FACTOR MEANS ▸
4H OB
4H Order Block. The zone (the last opposing candle) that caused a 4H structure break — a demand zone before a bullish break, a supply zone before a bearish break.
Why: it marks where big orders were likely left behind; price tends to return and react there. This is the base of every setup — price must be inside it to arm.
15m CHoCH / BOS
15m structure aligned. On the 15m, price broke structure the same way as your 4H OB. CHoCH (Change of Character) = the first break against the prior trend; BOS (Break of Structure) = a continuation break with the trend.
Why: it confirms lower-timeframe momentum has flipped to agree with your zone — the trigger that turns "price is in a zone" into an actual entry.
FVG
Fair Value Gap. A 3-candle imbalance where price moved so fast it left an untouched gap. The engine only counts meaningful, recent gaps near price.
Why: gaps act like magnets and support/resistance — an FVG in your direction shows strong one-sided momentum and often gives a precise entry as price fills back into it.
EQH / EQL
Equal Highs / Equal Lows. Two or more swing highs (or lows) resting at almost the same level near your zone.
Why: equal levels are where stop-losses pile up (resting liquidity). Price is often drawn to sweep them before reversing, which can fuel the move out of your zone.
Liquidity Sweep
Liquidity sweep (stop hunt). A candle that spiked through a prior swing high/low to grab stops, then got rejected and closed back on the original side (a long wick).
Why: it's smart money grabbing liquidity before reversing. A sweep into your zone is a strong sign the reversal is real, not just price drifting through.
Double Top / Bottom
Double top / double bottom. Two swing highs at an equal level (double top, bearish) or two swing lows at an equal level (double bottom, bullish), with a real pullback between them.
Why: a classic exhaustion pattern — price tried a level twice and failed, signalling the current move is running out and a reversal in your direction is likely.
Major S/R
Major support / resistance. Your 4H OB overlaps a strong horizontal S/R band that price has respected many times in history.
Why: an OB sitting on a major level is far stronger than one in open space — you have two independent reasons for price to react there.
Candle pattern
Candlestick reversal pattern. Morning/Evening Star, Engulfing, Hammer, Shooting Star, Piercing Line, Dark Cloud, Tweezer, Harami, Three Soldiers/Crows — detected on the 15m first, then the 4H.
Why: only counted when the pattern prints inside the order block and points the same way as the setup. A pattern in open space is noise; the same pattern at a level you were already watching is the market showing rejection right where you expected it — that is your entry trigger.
Daily / Weekly S/R
Higher-timeframe location. The 4H order block overlaps a support/resistance band built from daily or weekly candles — a level the market has respected for months, not hours.
Why: an OB is only as good as where it sits. A zone in open space has one reason to hold; a zone on a weekly level has two independent ones. When price is inside that band too, the card is flagged ★ PRIME LOCATION — the 4H setup and the higher-timeframe level agree, which is the strongest version of this setup.
SCORE / 10
Confluence score. 4H OB counts as the first point; each extra factor above adds one, up to 10. The dots on each card show the score.
Why: more factors stacking in the same direction = a higher-quality setup. Use the CONF filter (2+ / 3+ / 4+) to hide the thin ones.
